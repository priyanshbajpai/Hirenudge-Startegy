# HireNudge GTM and Product–Market Fit Plan

Working date: July 29, 2026  
Decision: which customer and outcome HireNudge should validate first, and how to acquire the first repeatable cohort without scaling ahead of product evidence.

## Bottom line

HireNudge should not take a broad “AI tools for every job seeker” proposition to market yet. The public product covers job matching, resume building and optimization, application tracking, recruiter outreach, cover letters, interview preparation, LinkedIn optimization, and autofill. That breadth makes the product look useful, but it does not identify the customer, urgent situation, or outcome for which HireNudge is the best choice.

The recommended first hypothesis is:

> For English-speaking Indian professionals with 1–5 years of experience who are actively switching private-sector roles, HireNudge turns one real job description into a truthful, tailored application pack and clear next action in under ten minutes.

The proposed longer-term differentiation is an India-first job-search operating system that learns from application outcomes and, for selected users, adds India-to-global mobility support through TerraTern. This is a strategic hypothesis. It must be tested against internal product data, customer behavior, willingness to pay, and founder priorities.

The next 30 days should establish a strong market-fit signal, not claim product–market fit. The proof threshold proposed for the first paid cohort is:

- 50 paid users;
- at least 35 completing the activation journey;
- at least 25 repeating the core workflow;
- day-seven core-action retention of at least 40%;
- refunds below 10%;
- one segment–message–channel combination worth repeating;
- early evidence that interview responses per qualified application improve from the user's baseline.

## What the public product currently communicates

### Verified from the live public site

The July 29 review found:

- a headline promise around applying smarter, tracking opportunities, and landing interviews faster;
- a “10,000+ job seekers” claim and employer-logo proof section;
- job matching and match percentages;
- an ATS resume scorer and resume builder;
- an email-connected application tracker;
- recruiter-email outreach;
- cover letter, interview preparation, LinkedIn optimization, and autofill surfaces;
- a free plan with 30 credits and ten outreach contacts;
- a Pro plan at $19 plus GST per month, displayed as $22.42 including GST;
- a Career Boost plan at $45 plus GST for three months, displayed as $53.10 including GST.

Public pages describe the audience as students, recent graduates, experienced professionals, career switchers, and job seekers worldwide. The product therefore presents a wide addressable market but no clear initial ICP.

### Public-site issues to verify internally

These observations require owner confirmation before they are described as product defects:

- Product images display `hirenudge.com` while the live service uses `hirenudge.ai`.
- “Auto-apply” and “autopilot” language can imply autonomous submission, while other copy describes user review and autofill.
- Pricing calls some features “unlimited” while use is constrained by credit balances.
- Pricing copy says annual billing is available, while the public plans reviewed show lifetime free, monthly Pro, and a three-month Career Boost plan.
- The same testimonial set is repeated across multiple feature pages.
- Public outcome claims, testimonials, employer logos, and the “10,000+” figure need an internal evidence and permission register.
- A headless unauthenticated review returned an empty signup-page body. This needs manual reproduction before it is classified as a conversion issue.

These are not only brand concerns. Trust and clarity influence resume uploads, inbox connection, payment, recruiter outreach, and continued use.

## Market-fit hypothesis

### Primary ICP to test

English-speaking Indian professionals who:

- have 1–5 years of experience;
- are employed or recently employed;
- intend to apply within the next 30 days;
- are targeting private-sector roles in technology, product, analytics, operations, marketing, consulting, or adjacent functions;
- have already applied manually or used a resume/job-search tool;
- care about better-fit interviews, not only higher application volume;
- can pay at least ₹499 for a guided 30-day job-search sprint.

### Comparison cohorts

- Final-year students and recent graduates.
- India-origin applicants targeting one international destination.

Keep these cohorts separate. Student acquisition may be easier while willingness to pay and urgency are lower. Cross-border applicants may have higher-value problems but require accurate mobility and work-authorization guidance.

### Anti-ICP for the first test

- People who are not applying within 30 days.
- Users seeking guaranteed employment.
- Users who primarily want high-volume autonomous applications.
- Applicants whose main need is immigration advice rather than job-search execution.
- People unwilling to review AI-generated claims before submission.

### High-stakes situation

The user is applying repeatedly but is uncertain which roles are genuinely winnable, how to tailor each application without inventing experience, and what to do after submission. Current alternatives are spreadsheets, generic resume tools, job boards, manual rewriting, and disconnected AI prompts.

### Proposed wedge

For one real job:

1. Verify role relevance and known blockers.
2. Explain fit using the user's evidence.
3. Produce a truthful, editable application pack.
4. Save the role and submitted version.
5. Recommend the next action.
6. Learn from the eventual response.

The wedge is application quality plus learning, not generic AI generation or application volume.

### Proof required

The hypothesis is supported only if users:

- bring real jobs into the workflow;
- save or submit the produced assets;
- return for a second qualified application;
- pay without requiring unsustainable founder intervention;
- report fewer corrections and greater confidence;
- produce employer responses at a better rate than their own baseline;
- refer similar active job seekers.

Survey enthusiasm or registrations alone are not market-fit evidence.

## Initial offer

### Founding Job Search Sprint

> Thirty days of HireNudge, ten truthful tailored application packs, application tracking, interview preparation, and one weekly group office hour.

Use an output guarantee:

> If a customer completes onboarding but cannot produce five usable, truthful application packs within 14 days, refund the purchase.

Do not guarantee interviews, offers, or employment.

### Price test

- Customers 1–20: ₹499.
- Customers 21–40: ₹999.
- Customers 41–50: ₹1,499 only if activation remains at or above 70% and refunds remain below 10%.

This ladder tests willingness to pay without confusing free testers with customers. Record founder time as part of customer acquisition and delivery cost.

## Go-to-market sequence

### 1. Founder and team network

Recruit narrowly matched people through direct, human outreach. The objective is fast objection learning and observed use, not reach.

Test:

- employed switcher message versus generic job-search message;
- application-quality promise versus all-in-one convenience;
- job-first onboarding versus resume-first onboarding.

### 2. Live application clinics

Run small sessions where attendees bring one real job description and resume. Demonstrate the complete workflow and invite qualified participants into the paid sprint.

Measure attendance-to-paid, paid-to-activated, time to first usable pack, and repeated use.

### 3. Consented TerraTern segment

Test only with purpose-specific consent and founder approval. Select people already considering international employment rather than broadcasting to the full lead base.

Measure whether mobility context materially increases activation, payment, or retention. Do not assume the TerraTern adjacency is valuable until behavior supports it.

### 4. Alumni, campus, coach, bootcamp, and community pilots

Partner with small groups that can recruit a defined cohort. Avoid free workshops that generate volume without activation. Give each partner a unique source code and review retention by partner.

### 5. Referral loop

Ask after the user creates and saves the first usable application pack. Test whether customers introduce another active job seeker immediately.

### 6. SEO and programmatic job discovery

Use SEO as a compounding channel after job quality and activation are understood. Connect high-intent pages to a specific workflow such as:

- tailor an application for this job;
- check sponsorship and work authorization;
- prepare notice-period and expected-CTC answers;
- compare a role with the user's evidence.

Do not treat indexed job-page volume as acquisition success without valid jobs, qualified signups, activation, and retention.

### 7. Paid acquisition

Delay material paid spend until one organic or partner-acquired segment demonstrates repeat use and acceptable refunds. Paid traffic should amplify a working loop, not diagnose basic product value.

## Thirty-day execution plan

### Days 1–3: establish reality

- Confirm founder objectives, target revenue, runway constraints, and available capacity.
- Pull registration, activation, usage, retention, payment, refund, and acquisition cohorts.
- Review support history and prior customer research.
- Verify public claims, pricing configuration, job sources, and product capabilities.
- Define the initial ICP, anti-ICP, and qualified-application standard.
- Instrument the job-first funnel.
- Observe ten users completing a real application.

Decision: whether the proposed segment and application-quality wedge are credible enough for a paid test.

### Days 4–7: first ten paid users

- Launch the ₹499 cohort.
- Onboard users with a real job rather than a dashboard tour.
- Get ten paid users and target seven activated.
- Fix only material trust, reliability, or activation blockers.
- Record every correction users make to generated content.
- Run the first live application clinic.

Decision: which message and onboarding path should continue into week two.

### Week 2: compare segments and messages

- Reach 25 paid users and target at least 17 activated.
- Compare employed switchers with students/recent graduates.
- Test job-first versus resume-first onboarding.
- Test “truthful application in ten minutes” versus “manage your whole search.”
- Conduct non-activation and refund interviews.

Decision: which ICP, message, and workflow deserve the next 25 customers.

### Week 3: test distribution and repetition

- Reach 40 paid users and target at least 27 activated.
- Test one consented TerraTern cohort if approved.
- Trigger referrals after first value.
- Run two partner or community pilots.
- Measure second qualified application and day-seven retention.

Decision: whether any channel brings activated and retained users without disproportionate founder effort.

### Week 4: choose, change, or stop

- Reach 50 paid, 35 activated, and 25 repeat users.
- Complete at least 25 deeper interviews, including non-activators and refunds.
- Calculate cash CAC, founder-time CAC, delivery effort, and model cost.
- Compare outcomes by persona, message, onboarding path, and source.
- Select the next six-week product and GTM focus.

Decision options:

- **Continue:** one segment and channel meet the proof thresholds.
- **Iterate:** the problem is strong but activation, workflow quality, or positioning is weak.
- **Change wedge:** users pay and engage around a different step, such as interview preparation or tracking.
- **Stop scaling:** payment, activation, repetition, or trust is too weak.

## Measurement

### Primary customer-outcome candidate

**Interview responses per qualified submitted application.**

This is more aligned with customer value than applications generated or sent. It should be compared with each user's recent baseline and interpreted carefully when sample sizes are small.

### Activation

Within 24 hours:

- profile substantially completed;
- one real job analysed;
- one truthful tailored asset saved;
- one application submitted or three qualified jobs tracked.

### Leading metrics

- qualified lead-to-paid conversion;
- median time to first usable application pack;
- paid-to-activated conversion;
- generated claims corrected or rejected;
- assets saved and confirmed submitted;
- second qualified application within seven days;
- referral request and referral conversion.

### Retention and business metrics

- day-seven and day-fourteen core-action retention;
- paid conversion by segment and source;
- refund and churn rate;
- cash CAC and founder-time CAC;
- model and infrastructure cost per activated user;
- weekly qualified applications completed.

### Guardrails

- fabricated or unsupported claim rate;
- irrelevant-application rate;
- stale or invalid job rate;
- unauthorized data sharing;
- outreach bounce, unsubscribe, complaint, and spam-report rate;
- autofill corrections and failed supported-site attempts;
- application volume without quality.

## Internal evidence required before finalizing the plan

1. Current active, retained, and paying users by segment and source.
2. Feature-level usage and paths associated with retention.
3. Pricing, credit consumption, refunds, and unit costs.
4. Match-score definition and evaluation evidence.
5. Extension installation, activation, supported-site success, and correction rates.
6. Job-source contracts, freshness, duplication, and removal rules.
7. Outreach contact sourcing, consent basis, delivery, response, and complaint data.
8. Support tickets, cancellations, and interview notes.
9. Evidence and permissions behind public adoption, employer-logo, testimonial, and outcome claims.
10. Product roadmap, engineering capacity, and founder objectives.

Any of these could change the recommendation. For example, if tracker users already retain much better than resume-tool users, HireNudge should test the tracker and outcome-learning loop as the primary wedge. If one existing paid segment materially outperforms the proposed ICP, follow the observed segment.

## Founder presentation

Use this concise framing:

> I reviewed the public product and the existing market work. HireNudge has broad feature coverage, but the public proposition is still too broad to show where we uniquely win. I recommend treating market fit as a hypothesis to validate, starting with active Indian professionals switching roles. The first promise to test is a truthful, tailored application and clear next action from one real job description in under ten minutes. I would validate it through a 30-day paid cohort, measuring activation, repeated use, refunds, and interview responses per qualified application. Before launching, I need the internal funnel, retention, pricing, support, roadmap, and claims evidence because those data may identify a better segment or product wedge.
