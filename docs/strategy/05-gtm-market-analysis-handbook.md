# GTM and Market Analysis for HireNudge

Working date: July 29, 2026  
Purpose: a practical learning handbook for understanding, diagnosing, and building HireNudge's go-to-market system.

## How to use this handbook

Do not read this as a list of marketing tactics. Work through it as a decision system:

1. Learn the idea.
2. Apply it to HireNudge.
3. Identify what is fact, inference, or assumption.
4. Run the smallest test that could change the decision.
5. Record the result and update the strategy.

By the end, you should be able to explain:

- which market HireNudge is actually entering;
- who should be the first ideal customer profile, or ICP;
- which urgent job the product should own;
- why a customer should choose HireNudge over a competitor or a manual workflow;
- which offer, price, channel, and onboarding path should be tested;
- how acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, and referral fit together;
- which metrics show customer value rather than activity;
- what evidence is required before increasing acquisition spend.

This handbook uses four evidence labels:

- **Verified:** directly observed in a reliable internal source or current public surface.
- **User-provided:** supplied by a founder or team member but not independently checked.
- **Inference:** a reasoned interpretation of evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** a claim that must be tested.

## 1. The central idea

Market analysis asks:

> Is there a sufficiently valuable problem, for a sufficiently reachable group, that HireNudge can solve better than the available alternatives?

Go-to-market, or GTM, asks:

> How will HireNudge repeatedly find those people, earn their trust, help them reach value, convert some to paid, retain them during the search, and turn outcomes into growth?

Product strategy asks:

> What should HireNudge build or improve so the promised outcome becomes true and defensible?

These are one connected system:

`Market → customer → problem → promise → product experience → proof → distribution → revenue → learning`

Weak teams start with channels. Strong teams start with a customer decision and a measurable outcome.

For HireNudge, the current decision is not “How do we get more traffic?” It is:

> Which job seeker, in which high-urgency situation, will pay for and repeatedly use which HireNudge workflow because it improves the quality of their search?

## 2. HireNudge's current strategic snapshot

### Verified from public surfaces on July 29, 2026

- HireNudge publicly offers job matching, resume building and optimization, job tracking, recruiter outreach, cover letters, interview preparation, LinkedIn optimization, and browser autofill.
- The homepage claims “10,000+ Job Seekers” and displays employer logos and outcome-oriented testimonials.
- Public pricing shows:
  - Free: 30 credits and 10 outreach contacts;
  - Pro: $19 plus 18% GST monthly, shown as $22.42 payable;
  - Career Boost: $45 plus 18% GST for three months, shown as $53.10 payable.
- The Chrome extension showed 8 users, one rating, version 7.3.0, and a July 11, 2026 update.
- The extension says it fills forms but leaves submission to the user.

### Inferences from the public product

- Feature breadth is close to Teal, Careerflow, Simplify, Huntr, and other horizontal job-search products.
- Broad feature parity does not tell a customer why HireNudge is the best choice.
- Resume, inbox, application, recruiter-contact, and work-authorization data make trust part of the product, not merely a legal requirement.
- A user who lands a role may naturally stop using the product, so HireNudge must design around a time-bounded search episode rather than assume conventional perpetual SaaS retention.

### Current strategic hypotheses

- The initial ICP should be English-speaking Indian professionals with 1–5 years of experience who are actively switching private-sector roles.
- The first promise to test should be:

  > Turn one real job description into a truthful, tailored application and clear next action in under ten minutes.

- The longer-term wedge could be an India-first job-search operating system with an India-to-global mobility layer.
- The primary customer-outcome candidate should be interview responses per qualified submitted application.
- The recurring product loop should be application quality plus outcome learning, not application volume.

### Evidence still missing

- signup, activation, feature-use, retention, paid, refund, and source cohorts;
- current pricing entitlements and unit costs;
- support tickets, cancellation reasons, and interview notes;
- match-score definition and evaluation results;
- job freshness, duplication, source, and removal quality;
- extension success and correction rates by supported site;
- outreach sourcing, bounce, reply, unsubscribe, and complaint data;
- evidence and permissions for public user, employer-logo, testimonial, and outcome claims;
- founder objectives, capacity, runway, and revenue targets.

No material GTM recommendation is final until this evidence is reviewed.

## Part I: Market analysis

## 3. Define the market correctly

A market is not “everyone who needs a job.” A useful market definition contains:

- a customer;
- a situation or trigger;
- a desired outcome;
- an alternative currently used;
- a reason the problem is worth solving now.

### Bad market definitions

- AI careers;
- job seekers;
- resume tools;
- HR technology.

They are too broad to guide product, pricing, or distribution.

### Better HireNudge market definition

> Digitally active Indian professionals conducting a serious private-sector job search, who need to choose better-fit roles, create truthful tailored applications, manage follow-up, and improve interview response.

### Strategic expansion definition

> Indian job seekers pursuing domestic or international opportunities who need an evidence-backed search workflow plus work-authorization and mobility guidance.

The first is a launch market. The second is a possible expansion market. Do not confuse future category ambition with the first customer to win.

## 4. Understand the customer's job to be done

A job to be done, or JTBD, describes the progress a customer is trying to make in a specific situation.

Use this structure:

> When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [desired progress], despite [barriers].

### HireNudge JTBD hypothesis

> When I decide to switch jobs and find a promising role, I want to know whether it is genuinely worth applying to and create a strong, truthful application quickly, so I can get more relevant interviews without turning my search into a second full-time job.

### Functional, emotional, and social jobs

| Job type | HireNudge example |
|---|---|
| Functional | Find suitable jobs, tailor materials, fill forms, track progress, follow up |
| Emotional | Feel less lost, less rejected, and more in control |
| Social | Present as credible, competent, and intentional to recruiters and peers |

A resume rewrite is a feature. “Know that I am sending a credible application to a winnable role” is progress.

### Research questions

Ask about recent behavior, not imagined use:

1. Tell me about the last role you applied to.
2. Show me how you found it and decided it was worth applying.
3. Show me every file, tab, prompt, and spreadsheet you used.
4. Where did you lose time or confidence?
5. What did you edit before submitting?
6. What have you already paid for?
7. What happened after submission?
8. What would you do if HireNudge did not exist?

Do not ask, “Would you use an AI job-search operating system?” That produces polite fiction.

## 5. Segment the market

Segmentation divides a broad market into groups with meaningfully different problems, behavior, economics, or routes to reach them.

Useful HireNudge dimensions include:

- search status: passive, preparing, active, urgent;
- career stage: student, early career, experienced, leadership;
- search type: first job, lateral move, career switch, return to work;
- geography: India domestic, India-to-one-country, already abroad;
- role family: engineering, product, analytics, operations, marketing, consulting;
- willingness to pay;
- search intensity and deadline;
- current workaround;
- distribution source;
- trust and data-sharing comfort.

### Candidate segment scorecard

Score each factor from 1 to 5 using evidence:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Pain severity | Does failure cost time, income, confidence, or opportunity? |
| Frequency | Does the problem occur repeatedly during an active search? |
| Urgency | Must the customer act within 30 days? |
| Willingness to pay | Has the customer paid for adjacent help or committed money now? |
| Reachability | Can the team identify and contact the segment cheaply? |
| Product fit | Can the current product deliver value without heavy custom work? |
| Retention potential | Will the customer repeat the core workflow during the search? |
| Strategic advantage | Does India or TerraTern context create a credible edge? |
| Outcome measurability | Can HireNudge observe value rather than rely on vague sentiment? |
| Risk | Can the workflow operate safely, truthfully, and compliantly? |

Do not add the scores mechanically and call the answer strategy. Use the scorecard to expose disagreement and missing evidence.

### Initial segments to compare

| Segment | Likely advantage | Main uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Employed Indian switchers, 1–5 years | Urgency, payment ability, repeated applications | Can HireNudge beat existing habits? |
| Final-year students and recent graduates | Reachable through campuses and communities | Lower willingness to pay and crowded needs |
| India-to-global applicants | High-value complexity and TerraTern adjacency | Smaller initial segment, accuracy and trust burden |

The anti-ICP matters as much as the ICP. Initially exclude people who are not applying soon, demand guaranteed placement, want unattended mass applications, or will not review AI-written claims.

## 6. Size the market

TAM, SAM, and SOM are decision tools, not decorative investor numbers.

- **TAM:** total addressable market if every relevant customer bought.
- **SAM:** serviceable available market that the current product, language, geography, and motion can serve.
- **SOM:** serviceable obtainable market that the team can realistically capture within a defined period.

### Use annual search episodes

Job search is episodic. One person may search in one year and not the next. Therefore:

`Annual market value = annual qualified search episodes × revenue per search episode`

The existing baseline models:

- India TAM: 19.0–27.5 million annual candidate episodes;
- Launch SAM: 5.4–11.8 million episodes;
- Year-three SOM: 2,675–23,620 paying users;
- willingness-to-pay assumption: ₹1,499–₹2,999 per episode.

These are scenario outputs, not audited forecasts.

### Bottom-up sizing

Bottom-up sizing is usually more useful for an early GTM:

`Reachable prospects × qualified rate × conversion × annual episodes × price`

Example:

`20 partner communities × 500 members × 20% active searchers × 10% paid conversion × ₹1,499`

`= ₹2,99,800 from 200 customers`

This does not prove demand. It makes the assumptions visible and testable.

### Sensitivity analysis

Change one uncertain input at a time:

- annual active-search rate;
- reachable share;
- paid conversion;
- price;
- refund rate;
- repeated purchase or referral rate.

If the business only works under the most optimistic combination, the market model is not decision-safe.

## 7. Analyze demand

Demand evidence becomes stronger as it moves from words to costly behavior:

1. broad interest;
2. problem acknowledgement;
3. recent workaround;
4. time spent;
5. data or workflow commitment;
6. referral or introduction;
7. deposit;
8. payment;
9. repeated use;
10. measurable outcome.

For HireNudge:

- a survey response is weak;
- uploading a resume and real job description is stronger;
- paying ₹499 is stronger still;
- returning for a second application is stronger than payment alone;
- showing a better interview-response rate is the strongest early product evidence.

### Demand questions to answer

- How often does the problem occur in a 30-day search?
- What does the user do today?
- What does that workaround cost in time, money, or lost interviews?
- Which step creates the most anxiety or abandonment?
- Who already pays for resume help, coaching, premium job boards, or immigration support?
- Which trigger creates urgency: resignation, layoff, graduation, notice period, visa deadline, or stalled career growth?

## 8. Map competitors and substitutes

Competitors are not only similar software.

### Direct competitors

Teal, Careerflow, Simplify, Huntr, Jobscan, Rezi, Kickresume, LoopCV, and emerging Indian job-search tools.

### Indirect competitors

- LinkedIn Premium;
- Naukri and other job boards;
- career coaches and resume writers;
- university placement cells;
- immigration consultants;
- recruitment agencies.

### Substitutes

- spreadsheets and Notion;
- ChatGPT or other general AI prompts;
- manually edited resume versions;
- friends, alumni, and mentors;
- doing nothing or applying with one generic resume.

### Competitor analysis template

For every competitor, record:

| Field | What to find |
|---|---|
| Primary customer | Who is the product visibly built for? |
| Core job | What urgent outcome does it emphasize? |
| Wedge | What first action or capability attracts users? |
| Habit loop | What brings users back? |
| Distribution | SEO, extension, community, partnerships, referrals, paid, B2B |
| Free value | What useful outcome is available before payment? |
| Price and packaging | Time period, limits, anchors, guarantees, renewal |
| Proof | Outcomes, users, reviews, security, institutions, extension installs |
| Trust | Legal identity, privacy, permissions, claims methodology |
| Weakness | What user problem remains unresolved? |
| Response | Copy, avoid, or differentiate |

### Avoid the feature-matrix trap

A feature matrix shows parity. Strategy requires understanding:

- why the customer starts;
- why they return;
- why they pay;
- why they trust the product;
- how the company acquires customers;
- what data or workflow compounds with use.

HireNudge does not win by adding the last missing checkbox. It wins by owning a narrower outcome and building evidence around it.

## 9. Evaluate market attractiveness

Assess:

- problem urgency;
- customer willingness to pay;
- frequency during the episode;
- market growth and technology shifts;
- competitor strength;
- ease of switching;
- distribution access;
- gross-margin potential;
- regulatory and trust burden;
- potential for data or workflow advantage.

### HireNudge market tensions

| Positive force | Counterforce |
|---|---|
| High job-search pain | Many free alternatives |
| Repeated workflow during active search | Search ends when the user succeeds |
| AI reduces creation time | AI-generated sameness reduces trust |
| Browser extension can create habit | Extension permissions and reliability create risk |
| India localization may improve fit | Localization alone is copyable |
| TerraTern may add mobility advantage | Data consent and brand coupling must be explicit |
| Large SEO demand | Thin pages and stale jobs can destroy trust |

The market is attractive only if HireNudge can connect workflow quality, trust, and distribution better than competitors.

## Part II: Go-to-market

## 10. Understand what GTM includes

GTM is not a launch campaign. It is the operating system that connects:

- ICP;
- positioning;
- product promise;
- offer and packaging;
- price;
- acquisition channels;
- activation;
- lifecycle and retention;
- sales or self-serve motion;
- partnerships;
- measurement;
- customer learning.

A GTM can produce many signups and still fail if users do not reach value or return.

## 11. Positioning

Positioning answers:

> For whom is HireNudge the best choice, in which situation, versus which alternative, because of what unique mechanism and proof?

### Positioning template

> For [ICP] who [high-stakes situation], HireNudge is the [category/reference frame] that [specific outcome]. Unlike [current alternative], it [unique mechanism]. This is credible because [proof].

### HireNudge test version

> For Indian professionals actively switching roles, HireNudge is a guided job-search workspace that turns each real job into a truthful, tailored application and next action in under ten minutes. Unlike generic resume tools or disconnected AI prompts, it connects fit, evidence, application, tracking, and outcome learning.

The last sentence is still a hypothesis until the product experience and outcomes prove it.

### Positioning hierarchy

- **Immediate promise:** one truthful application and next action in under ten minutes.
- **Product wedge:** qualified application workspace plus learning loop.
- **Strategic category:** India-first job-search operating system.
- **Expansion:** India-to-global eligibility and mobility support.

Do not lead with “AI-powered.” AI is an ingredient, not the customer outcome.

## 12. Build the offer

An offer combines:

- outcome;
- scope;
- time;
- format;
- support;
- price;
- risk reversal;
- eligibility.

### Founding Job Search Sprint

> Thirty days of HireNudge, ten truthful tailored application packs, tracking, interview preparation, and one weekly group office hour.

Possible output guarantee:

> If a customer completes onboarding but cannot produce five usable, truthful application packs within 14 days, refund the purchase.

Never guarantee interviews, offers, or employment. Employer decisions are outside HireNudge's control.

### Why a guided offer first

A guided cohort:

- collects payment;
- exposes workflow failures quickly;
- lets the team observe behavior;
- generates objections and language;
- separates product gaps from education gaps.

The risk is hiding poor product usability with founder labor. Track assistance time per customer and gradually remove it.

## 13. Pricing and packaging

Pricing is a market-research instrument before it is an optimization problem.

### Questions pricing must answer

- What outcome is the customer paying for?
- Is value best packaged by search episode, month, usage, or result?
- Which actions should remain free to create a useful habit?
- Do credits map to customer value or merely model cost?
- Does the customer understand limits before payment?
- Does the renewal period match the typical search duration?
- Which segment has higher willingness to pay?

### Initial price ladder

- customers 1–20: ₹499;
- customers 21–40: ₹999;
- customers 41–50: ₹1,499 only if activation and refunds remain healthy.

This is a willingness-to-pay test, not a permanent price.

### Pricing research methods

- real checkout tests;
- refundable deposits;
- package choice with actual payment;
- price increase across comparable cohorts;
- cancellation and refund interviews;
- Van Westendorp questions as secondary directional evidence;
- conjoint analysis later, when the product and sample are mature.

Do not infer willingness to pay from “₹999 sounds reasonable.”

## 14. Choose the GTM motion

Possible motions:

- self-serve product-led growth;
- founder-led or assisted sales;
- partner-led distribution;
- institutional B2B2C;
- content and SEO-led acquisition;
- paid acquisition.

### Recommended sequence for HireNudge

1. Founder-led paid cohort.
2. Live application clinics.
3. Referrals after first value.
4. Consented TerraTern pilot.
5. Small alumni, coach, bootcamp, campus, and community partnerships.
6. Product-connected SEO.
7. Paid acquisition only after activation and retention are healthy.

This sequence favors fast learning before scale.

## 15. Design channels as hypotheses

Every channel test needs:

- target segment;
- message;
- offer;
- activity volume;
- cost;
- conversion steps;
- activation and retention result;
- kill or continue threshold.

### Founder outreach

Hypothesis:

> A specific “bring one real job” message to employed switchers will create more paid and activated users than a generic all-in-one job-search message.

Measure:

- reply rate;
- qualified conversation rate;
- paid conversion;
- paid-to-activated;
- second application;
- founder time per activated user.

### Live clinics

The clinic should deliver the core workflow, not a presentation about AI.

Measure:

- registrations;
- attendance;
- qualified attendance;
- paid;
- activated;
- repeated use;
- referrals.

### TerraTern

Use purpose-specific consent. Test a narrowly relevant segment already considering international work.

Measure whether mobility context improves:

- message response;
- paid conversion;
- activation;
- retention;
- willingness to pay.

Do not assume that access to a lead list equals channel fit.

### Partnerships

Partner types include colleges, bootcamps, coaches, alumni groups, communities, and outplacement firms.

Measure each partner separately. A partner that generates 100 free signups and 2 activated users is weaker than one that generates 20 signups and 10 retained users.

### Referral

Ask after first value, not immediately after signup.

Trigger:

> The user saved a truthful application pack and can name the value received.

Measure invite rate, invite-to-qualified, qualified-to-paid, and retained referrals.

### SEO

SEO should connect intent to product action:

- tailor an application for this role;
- check work authorization or sponsorship;
- prepare notice-period and expected-CTC answers;
- compare a job with the user's evidence;
- create a country-specific application pack.

Page count is not success. Track valid job rate, qualified signup, activation, and retained use from each content cluster.

### Paid acquisition

Paid acquisition is useful only after:

- the audience is narrow;
- the promise converts;
- onboarding reaches value;
- repeated use exists;
- unit economics are measurable.

Otherwise, paid traffic purchases noise.

## 16. Build the funnel

A useful HireNudge funnel is:

`Qualified visitor → signup → real job imported → application pack saved → submitted or tracked → second qualified application → interview response → paid or renewed → referral`

### Define every stage

| Stage | Required event |
|---|---|
| Qualified visitor | Matches ICP and is applying within 30 days |
| Signup | Account created and source captured |
| Onboarding complete | Core profile and search constraints saved |
| First value | One real job analysed and truthful asset saved |
| Activated | First value plus submission or three qualified jobs tracked within 24 hours |
| Repeated | Second qualified application within seven days |
| Retained | Core action repeated in the defined weekly window |
| Outcome | Employer response, interview, offer, or other defined stage |
| Referred | Qualified invite accepted |

Never change metric definitions quietly. Maintain a metric dictionary.

## 17. Activation

Activation is the earliest behavior that predicts later customer value.

Current activation hypothesis:

> Within 24 hours, profile substantially complete + one real job analysed + one truthful tailored asset saved + one application submitted or three qualified jobs tracked.

Test whether this behavior actually predicts:

- second application;
- day-seven use;
- payment;
- interview response.

### Time to value

Target median time to first usable application pack: under ten minutes.

Measure time from the user's first meaningful action, not from a conveniently chosen backend event.

### Common activation failure modes

- generic dashboard tour;
- too much profile setup before value;
- low-quality or irrelevant job matching;
- AI output requiring heavy correction;
- unclear credits;
- missing trust at resume or inbox connection;
- extension failure;
- no obvious next action.

## 18. Retention in an episodic product

Conventional monthly retention can mislead because a successful user exits after landing a job.

Measure:

- second qualified application within seven days;
- active-search weekly retention;
- workflow depth during the episode;
- milestone progression;
- time to outcome;
- reactivation in a later search;
- referral after success.

Segment users into:

- still searching;
- paused;
- hired;
- churned without outcome;
- unknown.

“No longer active because hired” is not the same as churn due to low value.

## 19. Customer outcome and north-star metric

### North-star candidate

> Weekly qualified applications completed with HireNudge.

A qualified application should:

- meet the user's hard constraints;
- use a valid, fresh job;
- meet the segment's fit standard;
- contain reviewed, truthful material;
- be confirmed as submitted.

### Primary outcome candidate

> Interview responses per qualified submitted application.

Formula:

`Qualified interview response rate = qualified interview responses ÷ qualified submitted applications`

Keep the denominator visible. Compare with the user's recent baseline when possible.

### Guardrails

- fabricated-claim rate;
- irrelevant-application rate;
- stale or invalid job rate;
- autofill correction and failure rate;
- unauthorized data sharing;
- outreach bounce, unsubscribe, complaint, and spam rate;
- application volume without quality.

## 20. Unit economics

Key formulas:

`Cash CAC = acquisition cash spend ÷ new paying customers`

`Founder-time CAC = acquisition hours × loaded hourly cost ÷ new paying customers`

`Gross profit per customer = revenue − payment fees − model cost − delivery cost − support cost`

`Contribution margin = gross profit ÷ revenue`

`CAC payback period = CAC ÷ monthly gross profit per customer`

For an episodic product, a simple search-episode contribution model may be more useful than an inflated lifetime value:

`Episode contribution = episode revenue − variable acquisition and service costs`

If using LTV:

`LTV = average gross profit per paid episode × expected paid episodes × referral adjustment`

Do not assume recurring lifetime value without evidence that users renew, return for a later search, or refer others.

## 21. Experiment design

Use this template:

- **Decision:** what decision will this result change?
- **Hypothesis:** if we do X for Y, Z will improve because of mechanism M.
- **Segment:** exact inclusion and exclusion rules.
- **Control/comparison:** what is the alternative?
- **Primary metric:** one success measure.
- **Guardrails:** what must not worsen?
- **Sample and duration:** enough exposure to interpret.
- **Threshold:** continue, iterate, or stop rule.
- **Instrumentation:** events and properties required.
- **Result:** numbers and qualitative evidence.
- **Decision:** what changes now?

### Example

- **Decision:** which onboarding should be used for the next 25 paid users?
- **Hypothesis:** job-first onboarding will improve 24-hour activation because users see relevant value before completing a long profile.
- **Comparison:** job-first versus resume-first.
- **Primary metric:** paid-to-activated within 24 hours.
- **Guardrails:** unsupported-claim corrections, time to first value, refunds.
- **Threshold:** continue job-first if activation improves materially without worse correction or refund rates.

With small samples, report raw counts and confidence ranges. Do not call a 6-versus-4 result a universal truth.

## 22. Research and analytics operating system

### Weekly inputs

- funnel by segment and source;
- activation and repeat-use cohorts;
- five reviewed customer journeys;
- support and cancellation themes;
- experiment readouts;
- claims, privacy, and quality incidents;
- competitor changes;
- roadmap constraints.

### Friday decision memo

1. Bottom line.
2. Decisions required.
3. Evidence and confidence.
4. Top three priorities with owner and date.
5. Risks and dependencies.
6. What would change the recommendation.
7. Decisions explicitly deferred.

Do not produce a dashboard without a decision owner and action.

## 23. First-50 proof system

The proposed 30-day threshold is:

- 50 paid users;
- at least 35 activated;
- at least 25 repeating the workflow;
- day-seven core-action retention of at least 40%;
- refunds below 10%;
- one repeatable segment-message-channel combination;
- early outcome evidence against the user's baseline.

These thresholds are management choices, not laws of nature.

### Cohort design

- 30 employed switchers;
- 15 students or recent graduates;
- 5 India-to-global applicants.

Keep results separate. A blended average can hide the winning and losing segment.

### Decision at day 30

- **Continue:** payment, activation, repetition, and channel quality are strong.
- **Iterate:** the problem is real, but workflow or message is weak.
- **Change wedge:** a different product step drives value.
- **Stop scaling:** demand, trust, repeated use, or economics are insufficient.

## Part III: What to learn by doing

## 24. Six-week mastery plan

### Week 1: Market and customer

Learn:

- market definition;
- segmentation;
- JTBD;
- demand evidence;
- TAM, SAM, SOM.

Do:

- review five recent customer journeys;
- interview five active job seekers;
- build one segment scorecard;
- recreate the market model with explicit assumptions.

Output:

- one-page market definition;
- ICP and anti-ICP;
- evidence-gap register.

### Week 2: Competition and positioning

Learn:

- direct competitors, substitutes, and alternatives;
- wedge, habit loop, distribution engine, and proof;
- positioning and category design.

Do:

- use Teal, Simplify, Careerflow, and HireNudge for one real job;
- record onboarding, time to value, output corrections, pricing, and trust;
- write three positioning options.

Output:

- competitor battlecards;
- “copy, avoid, differentiate” memo;
- message-test plan.

### Week 3: Offer and pricing

Learn:

- offer construction;
- packaging;
- willingness-to-pay research;
- guarantees and risk reversal.

Do:

- sell the first version before adding features;
- run real checkout conversations;
- record objections and assistance time.

Output:

- founding offer;
- pricing ladder;
- objection library.

### Week 4: Funnel, activation, and retention

Learn:

- event taxonomy;
- activation criteria;
- cohort analysis;
- episodic retention.

Do:

- inspect raw event paths;
- define each funnel event;
- compare activated and non-activated users;
- observe failed onboarding.

Output:

- metric dictionary;
- funnel;
- activation diagnosis;
- retention cohorts.

### Week 5: Channels and economics

Learn:

- channel-market fit;
- founder-led, partner, referral, SEO, and paid motions;
- CAC, contribution, and payback.

Do:

- run one founder-outreach test;
- run one live clinic;
- trigger referrals after value;
- calculate cash and founder-time CAC.

Output:

- channel scorecard;
- unit-economics model;
- next-channel decision.

### Week 6: Experiments and operating cadence

Learn:

- hypothesis design;
- comparison groups;
- sample limits;
- decision thresholds;
- weekly synthesis.

Do:

- review all first-50 evidence;
- identify the highest-confidence segment, message, and workflow;
- write the next six-week plan.

Output:

- experiment readouts;
- founder decision memo;
- continue, iterate, change-wedge, or stop-scaling decision.

## 26. Common GTM mistakes for HireNudge

1. Treating all job seekers as one market.
2. Leading with an all-in-one feature list.
3. Calling signups or application volume growth.
4. Scaling ads before activation and retention are understood.
5. Treating SEO page count as authority.
6. Copying competitor features without understanding their distribution or habit loop.
7. Mixing student, employed, and cross-border cohorts.
8. Using surveys as willingness-to-pay evidence.
9. Ignoring founder time in CAC.
10. Assuming conventional SaaS retention in an episodic category.
11. Hiding poor usability with high-touch service.
12. Using TerraTern data without explicit purpose-specific consent.
13. Making outcome, testimonial, employer-logo, or user-count claims without evidence.
14. Encouraging high-volume outreach or applications without quality and complaint guardrails.
15. Reporting averages without denominators, cohort definitions, or sample sizes.

## 27. Mastery test

You understand HireNudge GTM and market analysis when you can answer these without hand-waving:

1. What exact event makes someone enter HireNudge's market?
2. Which first segment has the strongest pain, urgency, payment ability, and reachability?
3. What does that segment do today?
4. What is the narrow outcome HireNudge should own?
5. What evidence would falsify the proposed ICP?
6. What is HireNudge's wedge, habit loop, and possible moat?
7. Why would a user choose HireNudge instead of Teal, Simplify, ChatGPT, or a spreadsheet?
8. Which public claims are verified internally?
9. What is the actual time to first value?
10. Which activation behavior predicts a second qualified application?
11. Why does a retained user return?
12. What does a qualified application mean?
13. What is the interview-response rate per qualified application?
14. Which channel creates the most activated and retained users per rupee and founder hour?
15. What is the contribution margin per search episode?
16. Which segment-message-channel combination should receive the next 100 prospects?
17. What must be true before paid acquisition begins?
18. Which risk could most damage user trust?
19. What decision will this week's experiment change?
20. What evidence would make the team stop scaling or choose a different wedge?

If the answer is unknown, that is not failure. Write the uncertainty down and design the test.

## 29. Current conclusion

HireNudge does not need more generic GTM activity. It needs sharper market truth.

The current best hypothesis is to serve active Indian professionals switching roles, promise one truthful and tailored application plus a next action in under ten minutes, and validate that promise with a paid first-50 cohort. The business should measure repeated qualified applications and interview responses, keep trust and application quality as guardrails, and postpone broad acquisition until one segment, message, workflow, and channel work together.

The India-to-global operating-system story is strategically interesting because TerraTern may add mobility expertise, distribution, and service escalation. It becomes a strategy only after consented distribution, user demand, product quality, and willingness to pay are demonstrated.
